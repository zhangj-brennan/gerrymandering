How Tennessee's population growth compares against the ideal population

The ideal population of a county is the population of each county within a state if they were all equal in population size. In 1900, the ideal population per county for Tennessee was 20,410.
Some counties are larger in population, some smaller. In 1900, they ranged between 129,483 above and 14,704 below.
As population grew, the ideal population also grew.
But real population changes very differently. While some 17% of counties' growth aligns with that of the ideal population numbers,
about 69% of the counties did not keep up with that growth,
and the rest grew much faster,
making these counties' distributions and representation very different and much more unequal than in 1900.
